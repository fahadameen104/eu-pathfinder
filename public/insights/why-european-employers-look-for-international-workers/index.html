What to consider

Labour and skills shortages affect parts of Europe, but demand varies by country, occupation and employer. A shortage headline should be a starting point for research, not proof that a vacancy or work route is available to a particular applicant.

For workers from India, Bangladesh, Nepal and Pakistan, a useful first step is to describe actual experience, training and languages, then compare a named role with the destination’s requirements. Employers need to assess candidates against the work they will perform.

EU PathFinder Agency provides guidance and employer-matching support. Any recruitment or placement work must be set out in a separate written agreement. Hiring and immigration decisions remain with employers and authorities.

Your preparation checklist

  • Identify a specific occupation and destination.
  • Verify a named employer and a current written role.
  • Check work-authorisation requirements independently.

Check the official information

  • Council of the European Union: skills shortages and human capital
  • European Union: immigration information

For a particular application, follow the destination government or embassy’s current instructions. Rules, eligibility and application locations depend on your circumstances. For non-EU destinations, use that country’s own immigration authority.

Turn your questions into a plan

Tell us your nationality, current residence, occupation, experience and the destination you are considering. Keep sensitive documents out of the initial enquiry.

Contact us for current pricing. Recruitment or placement applies only under a separate written agreement. No job, visa or permit outcome is guaranteed.
